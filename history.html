The History of Video Games

Video games have evolved and changed dramatically from their early years. The first game, Pong was an experiment with basic graphics and controls, while newer games have expansive worlds, stories, and online communities behind them, the history of video games is an excellent example of how quickly technology can develop.

The Beginning - 1970s

Video games started to appear in arcades around the 1970s. Games were simple, introducing people to this new form of interactive entertainment. Many games had very simple graphics and controls, such as Pong and Space Invaders. The designers of games like Space Invaders knew their games were simple, so they made the gameplay more strategic and challenging to make the games more fun.

Home Consoles - 1980s

In order for video games to become as popular as they are today, they needed to become accessible to more people. The biggest home console in the early days of video games was the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES), which helped keep the video game industry alive. This system also introduced many characters and franchises that have become staples of the gaming world like characters from The Legend of Zelda.

3D Games - 1990s

3D graphics were a huge leap for video games. Becoming more common and consoles that were powerful enough to run these 3D graphics becoming more accessible to people, like the PlayStation and the Nintendo 64, allowed developers to start creating detailed worlds and more complex games like Super Mario 64 and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. Ocarina of Time is also widely considered one of the greatest video games ever made.

Online Games - 2000s

Internet connections became a staple of households, and changed how people play the games. Consoles like the Xbox and PlayStation started to support online multiplayer in some of their games. PC gaming also grew alongside console online gaming, with massive multiplayer games becoming increasingly popular.

With services like Xbox Live or the PlayStation network, people could play games with people without sitting in the same room.

Mobile Gaming and Digital Gaming - 2010s

With technology becoming more powerful, more energy efficient, and smaller, games began to become fully digital. Without needing a disc, cartridge, or any other physical media to play and own, games became more accessible being easier to purchase and to play. This also helped the world of online gaming through more people being able to buy more games, leading to more players in nearly every title.

The world of gaming also expanded to phones. Mobile games became an instant hit with games like Flappy Bird taking the world by storm and massive titles such as Fortnite also releasing in this period of time.

Modern Gaming - Present

Modern games have continued to become more powerful, more expansive, bigger, and more complex. With access to extremely powerful hardware developers are able to make complex graphics that continue to become more realistic and detailed as time goes on. Many games have also started to become cross platform, meaning that whether you and your friends are on PlayStation, Nintendo, Xbox, PC, or anything else, the world of gaming can be experienced together. Many other ways to experience games have also been developed like Virtual Reality or Augmented reality which try to make players feel more in the game than a regular TV or monitor could. Games now are the reason millions of people are connected and games have formed strong communities on their own, such as Fortnite which stays around 110 million active players each day.
